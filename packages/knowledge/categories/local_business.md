# Category playbook: Local business (shops, restaurants, clinics, trades, services)

## Where this category wins

Local marketing is a search-and-proximity game first and a social game second. Ranked by return per hour:

1. **Google Business Profile** - by a wide margin. This is where "[category] near me" converts, where your
   hours and address live, and where reviews accumulate. For most local businesses, GBP + reviews is
   80% of the outcome. Everything else is supporting cast.
2. **Reviews** (Google first, then the vertical ones: TripAdvisor, Zap/Rest for Israeli restaurants,
   Booksy/Treatwell for beauty, Doctolib/clinic directories for health). Review count, velocity, rating and
   your replies are the single biggest lever on local ranking and on conversion.
3. **Instagram** - the shop window. Food, beauty, fitness, retail, events, interiors: the audience is local,
   visual and ready to walk in. Location tags matter more than hashtags.
4. **Facebook (Page + local groups)** - still the strongest platform for 30-65 audiences and for community
   trust. **Local groups** are where recommendations happen, and they are the highest-intent, most
   rule-bound surface here.
5. **WhatsApp Business / Telegram channel** - the owned, no-algorithm channel. For repeat-purchase
   businesses (bakeries, salons, clinics, restaurants) a WhatsApp broadcast list or Telegram channel
   outperforms every social platform per message.
6. **TikTok** - increasingly a local discovery engine ("near me" searches happen on TikTok now). Excellent
   for food, beauty, fitness, and anything visually satisfying; the production bar is low.
7. **Google Maps photos** - not a "post" but the most-viewed asset on the profile. Upload real photos
   weekly.
8. **Waze / Apple Business Connect / Bing Places** - one-time setup, permanent value. Do them once.
9. **Local press, neighbourhood newsletters, municipal event calendars** - free, high trust, low frequency.
10. **Pinterest** - only for visually-output businesses (interiors, events, florists, bakeries) and mostly
    for non-local reach.
11. **LinkedIn** - only for B2B local services (accountants, lawyers, IT, commercial trades).
12. **Reddit / Hacker News / Product Hunt / Steam / itch.io / X / Bluesky / Threads** - skip. (Exception: a
    city subreddit's recommendation threads, where you answer only when genuinely asked and always disclose.)

## Communities

Each entry lists **the rule that gets you removed if you ignore it**.

### Google / directories
| Surface | Key rule |
|---|---|
| **Google Business Profile** | **Never add keywords or locations to the business name** (top suspension cause). Never solicit, incentivise or gate reviews. Post weekly; keep hours (including holiday hours) accurate. |
| **Google Maps Q&A** | You may seed and answer the real questions customers ask - that is allowed and useful. Answering as the business shows a badge; do not answer as a fake customer. |
| **Apple Business Connect / Bing Places / Waze** | One-time claim and verification. Keep NAP (name, address, phone) **byte-identical** across all of them - citation consistency is a real local ranking factor. |
| **Vertical directories** (TripAdvisor, Yelp, Booksy, Treatwell, Zap, Rest, Mitchell, Easy) | Each has its own review policy; Yelp in particular penalises asking for reviews at all. Read the one that matters in your vertical and follow it literally. |

### Facebook
| Community | Key posting rule |
|---|---|
| **Your Page** | No restriction, but low organic reach. Its real jobs: credibility, reviews/recommendations, events, and the entry point to boosting. |
| **Local city / neighbourhood groups** | The highest-value and most rule-bound surface. **Almost all restrict business posts to one designated day or a pinned weekly thread.** Read the pinned rules; posting outside the window is a permanent removal. **Manual, human-approved, one group at a time, never the same text twice.** |
| **"Recommendations" / "Ask the neighbourhood" groups** | Do **not** post about yourself. Answer when someone asks for your category, disclose that it is your business, and be brief. One reply, no follow-up pitch. |
| **Buy/sell/swap groups** | Only where the group explicitly permits business listings. |
| **Interest groups** (parents, pets, runners, new-in-town) | Same one-day-a-week pattern. Value first: answer questions for weeks before posting anything of your own. |

### Messaging
| Channel | Key rule |
|---|---|
| **WhatsApp Business** | Catalogue, business hours, away messages, labels. **Broadcast lists only reach people who have your number saved.** Requires opt-in; Israeli spam law (Amendment 40) and GDPR both require explicit consent and an opt-out. Never add people to a group without asking. |
| **WhatsApp Channels** | One-to-many, no reply, no phone-number exposure. Good for offers and closures. |
| **Telegram channel** | Owned broadcast. 1 post/day maximum - every post is a push notification. Never promote in groups you do not own. |
| **SMS** | Only with opt-in and an unsubscribe line. High cost, high open rate, easy to abuse. |

### Local press and events
| Surface | Key rule |
|---|---|
| **Municipal event calendars, community centre boards, local newsletters** | Free listings. Submit events 2-3 weeks ahead with a photo. |
| **Local news sites and neighbourhood blogs** | Pitch a *story*, not a promotion: an anniversary, a new dish, a hire, a community initiative, a closure. |
| **Neighbouring businesses** | Cross-promotion is the cheapest local marketing that exists: shared posts, a shelf swap, a joint event. Tag each other on Instagram/Facebook. |

## Launch sequence (first 30 days)

Day 0 assumption: the business exists (or opens within the month) and has a physical address or a defined
service area.

**Days 1-5 - claim and complete everything**
- Day 1: **Claim and verify the Google Business Profile.** Correct primary category (the most specific
  accurate one), up to 9 secondary categories, services/menu items listed individually, 750-character
  description, full hours including holiday hours, attributes (accessibility, parking, languages, outdoor
  seating, payment methods).
- Day 1: Upload **20 real photos**: exterior (so people recognise the door), interior, product, team, at
  work. 1200x900, no text overlays, no phone numbers in images.
- Day 2: Claim Apple Business Connect, Bing Places, Waze. NAP identical everywhere, character for character.
- Day 2: Vertical directory for your category (TripAdvisor / Booksy / Zap / etc.).
- Day 3: Facebook Page: category, hours, address, services, cover photo, "Message" button, Recommendations
  enabled.
- Day 3: Instagram business account linked to the Page; bio with what/where/hours and a link.
- Day 4: WhatsApp Business set up: catalogue, greeting message, away message, hours. Put the wa.me link in
  every bio.
- Day 5: Seed 8-10 real Q&A entries on the GBP (parking, accessibility, kashrut/dietary, languages spoken,
  walk-ins, payment).

**Days 6-14 - reviews and the first content rhythm**
- Day 6: **Ask your first 15 customers for a Google review.** In person or by message, with a short link
  (GBP gives you one). Never offer a discount for it - that is a policy violation.
- Day 7: GBP Update post #1 (what's new this week). Weekly from here on.
- Day 8: Instagram post #1 (4:5, real photo, location tagged) + a Story.
- Day 9: Facebook Page post #1 (same content, rewritten - never the identical text).
- Day 10: Join 3-5 local Facebook groups. **Read their pinned rules and note their business-post day.**
  Participate, do not post about yourself.
- Day 11: TikTok/Reel #1 - 15-25s, a real moment in the business, shot on a phone.
- Day 12: 5 more photos to GBP. Reply to every review received so far, in the review's language.
- Day 13: GBP Q&A: answer anything that came in; add any new question customers actually asked.
- Day 14: Start the WhatsApp/Telegram list: a sign at the counter and a line in every bio ("we post
  closures and the weekly specials there").

**Days 15-22 - local community and proof**
- Day 15: First post in a local Facebook group **on that group's designated business day**, with approval.
  One group only, written for that group.
- Day 16: GBP Update post #2 + Instagram post #2.
- Day 17: Reel/TikTok #2.
- Day 18: Contact 2-3 neighbouring businesses about a cross-promotion. Tag each other.
- Day 19: GBP **Offer or Event** post if you have a genuine, time-bounded one. Post it 5-10 days before it
  starts.
- Day 20: Ask the next 15 customers for reviews. Target: 25-30 total by day 30.
- Day 21: Instagram post #3 + Story poll ("which should we bring back?").
- Day 22: Pitch one local news site or neighbourhood newsletter with an actual story.

**Days 23-30 - consolidate**
- Day 23: GBP Update post #3. Photos again (weekly cadence, 3-5 each time).
- Day 24: Reel/TikTok #3, based on which of the first two performed.
- Day 25: Second local Facebook group, different group, different text, on its own business day.
- Day 26: Reply to **every** review, including the bad ones - calmly, factually, offering a fix offline.
- Day 27: Check GBP Insights: search queries used to find you, direction requests, calls, website clicks.
- Day 28: Adjust the GBP description and services to include the query words people actually used.
- Day 29: First WhatsApp/Telegram broadcast to the list (only if it has something worth a notification).
- Day 30: Set the ongoing cadence: **1 GBP post/week, 3-5 GBP photos/week, 2-3 Instagram posts/week, 1-2
  Reels/week, 1 Facebook group post per allowed day, reviews asked continuously, every review answered
  within 48 hours.**

## Content pillars that work

1. **What's available now.** The most useful and most ignored pillar. Today's specials, what is in stock,
   what sold out, today's wait time, this week's hours.
   - Angles: "the winter menu is up"; "we're out of X until Thursday"; "quiet right now if you want to come
     in"; holiday hours.
2. **The people and the craft.** Faces, hands, process. The thing that makes a local business not a chain.
   - Angles: a 20-second process clip; the team member who has been here 9 years; the supplier you buy from;
     6am prep.
3. **Practical answers.** Everything a customer has to ask before they come.
   - Angles: parking; accessibility; which door; dietary/kashrut; what to bring; how long it takes; what it
     costs.
4. **Community and place.** The neighbourhood, the neighbours, the local events.
   - Angles: cross-promoting the shop next door; a local event you host or sponsor; "new in the area? here's
     what's good on this street"; a customer's story with permission.
5. **Proof.** Reviews, before/afters, results, repeat customers.
   - Angles: a review screenshot (with permission) and your reply; a before/after of a repair or a
     treatment; "you asked for this and it's back".

Weekly mix: 2 x pillar 1 (GBP + Instagram), 1 x pillar 2 (the Reel), 1 x pillar 3 or 4, pillar 5 whenever
proof arrives.

## KPIs and realistic benchmarks

| Metric | Weak | OK | Good | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Google reviews, month 1** | <5 | 15-30 | 50+ | The single most predictive local metric. |
| **Google rating** | <4.0 | 4.3-4.6 | 4.7+ | Below 4.0 costs you the click even if you rank. |
| Review velocity (ongoing) | <1/mo | 4-10/mo | 20+/mo | Velocity matters as much as total. |
| Review response rate | <50% | 80% | 100% within 48h | Visible to every future customer. |
| **GBP monthly views (small local)** | <500 | 2,000-8,000 | 20,000+ | Varies hugely by category and city density. |
| GBP views → actions (calls, directions, clicks) | <3% | 5-10% | 15%+ | The conversion metric that matters. |
| Direction requests/month | <20 | 100-400 | 1,000+ | The closest proxy to footfall. |
| Calls from GBP/month | <5 | 20-80 | 200+ | Higher for services, lower for retail. |
| "Discovery" vs "Direct" search split | <20% discovery | 40-60% discovery | 70%+ discovery | Discovery = people who did not search your name. This is the growth number. |
| Instagram local reach/post | <200 | 500-2,000 | 5,000+ | With a location tag, in a dense city. |
| Instagram → visits | hard to attribute | - | - | Use a Story link sticker with a UTM, or a code mentioned only there. |
| Facebook group post (local, 10k members) | <10 reactions | 30-100 | 300+ | A good group post generates more calls than a month of Page posts. |
| WhatsApp/Telegram list size, month 3 | <50 | 200-600 | 1,500+ | Open rates of 60-90% make this the best-converting channel you have. |
| Repeat-visit rate | <20% | 30-45% | 60%+ | For most local businesses the whole game. |

Reality check: month 1 for a new local business is about **infrastructure and reviews**, not reach. Getting
to 25 genuine Google reviews at 4.6+, with a complete profile and weekly photos, beats any amount of
social posting.

## Common mistakes

1. **Keyword-stuffing the Google business name** ("Best Plumber Tel Aviv 24/7"). The most common cause of
   profile suspension.
2. **Incentivising or gating reviews** ("10% off for a 5-star review", or only asking happy customers).
   A Google policy violation with suspension risk, and Yelp penalises asking at all.
3. **Wrong or missing holiday hours.** The top local-search complaint everywhere, and in Israel it means
   setting special hours for every chag, not just the obvious ones.
4. **Never replying to reviews**, or replying defensively to a bad one. Every future customer reads your
   reply to the worst review, not the review itself.
5. **Posting the same text to five local Facebook groups.** Permanent removal from all five, and it is the
   single fastest way to become "that business" in a neighbourhood.
6. **Posting in a local group outside its designated business day.** Read the pinned rules. Every group has
   them, and every group enforces them.
7. **Stock photos.** Locals recognise them instantly and they defeat the purpose. Real phone photos of the
   real place outperform professional stock every time.
8. **Text and phone numbers baked into GBP post images.** Against Google's photo guidelines.
9. **Inconsistent NAP** across Google, Apple, Waze, Facebook and directories. Citation consistency is a real
   ranking input; a moved comma matters.
10. **Treating Instagram followers as the goal.** A local business needs 400 people who live within 3km, not
    8,000 followers in another country. Location tag everything.
11. **Not collecting a WhatsApp/Telegram list.** It is the only channel you own and the only one with a 70%+
    open rate.
12. **Ignoring Google Maps photos.** They are the most-viewed asset on the profile by a wide margin and cost
    nothing but a weekly five minutes.
13. **Announcing offers you will not honour** (a "limited time" that never ends), which is both a Google
    policy issue and a trust issue.
14. **Posting customer photos without consent**, especially children and medical/aesthetic before-afters.

## Israeli context (optional subsection)

Use when the business is in Israel or serves a Hebrew-speaking audience.

**Language.** Run the content in **Hebrew** by default; add English (and Russian or Arabic where the
customer base warrants) as a **separate** post, never mixed in one body. Set the GBP description in Hebrew
and add English as an additional language. Right-to-left text breaks some scheduling tools - check the
rendered post, especially where Hebrew and Latin characters or numbers are mixed.

**The week.** The working week is **Sunday to Thursday**. Sunday behaves like Monday elsewhere: **Sunday
morning is the prime posting slot** and the best day for GBP Updates and Facebook Page posts. Friday is a
half day (most businesses close 13:00-15:00). Saturday is quiet until sunset; **Saturday night (מוצ״ש) is a
strong window** for restaurants, entertainment and retail. Post weekend offers on **Wednesday or Thursday
morning**, not Friday.

**Holidays.** Set **special hours in GBP for every chag** - Rosh Hashanah, Yom Kippur, Sukkot, Pesach,
Shavuot, plus the intermediate days (chol hamoed), when opening hours are unusual and search traffic spikes.
Wrong holiday hours generate 1-star reviews. Pesach in particular changes what food businesses can sell and
is worth a dedicated post. Note that Israeli holiday dates move against the Gregorian calendar each year -
never hardcode them.

**Facebook groups are the dominant local channel in Israel.** Neighbourhood and city groups
("שכונת ...", "תושבי ...", "ממליצים ב...") carry more local commercial weight than anywhere else, and
recommendation threads ("מחפשת המלצה ל...") are the main way service businesses get customers. Rules are
strict and enforced by volunteer admins: most groups allow business posts on one specific day, in one pinned
thread, or only after an admin approves. **Always manual, always human-approved, one group at a time.**
Answering a recommendation request is the highest-value action available - reply once, disclose that it is
your business, keep it short.

**WhatsApp is the default channel.** Israeli customers expect to book, ask and order over WhatsApp.
WhatsApp Business with a catalogue and quick replies is close to mandatory; a broadcast list or Channel for
closures and specials outperforms Instagram for repeat business. Consent is required - **Israeli spam law
(Amendment 40 to the Communications Law) requires prior opt-in and a working opt-out for commercial
messages**, with statutory damages for violations. Never add a customer to a group without asking.

**Local review and booking platforms** matter alongside Google: **Zap / Rest / Mitchell** for restaurants,
**Easy / Booksy / Mytor** for beauty and services, **Doctors' directories** for clinics. Claim the ones in
your vertical.

**Practical details Israeli customers search for:** parking (חניה), accessibility (נגישות), kashrut
certification and which rabbinate, whether you are open on Shabbat, delivery apps you are on (Wolt, Tenbis,
Yango), and payment methods (Bit is expected). Put every one of these in the GBP attributes and answer them
in the Q&A - they are the questions that decide whether someone comes.
